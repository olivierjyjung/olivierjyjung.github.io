\section{Research Interests}
My research seeks to understand human communication and interaction through speech. I study spoken interaction from two complementary directions: \textbf{what} is said, through language and LLM-based analysis, and \textbf{how} it is said, through prosody and timing. By integrating linguistic and acoustic signals, I develop interpretable computational models of spoken interaction, from depth and responsiveness in facilitated group dialogue to early traces of cognitive and affective decline in how a person talks; through behavioral experiments, I also examine how trust and memory shift when a voice turns out to be synthetic. Working across \textbf{Speech and Language Processing}, \textbf{Human-AI Interaction}, and \textbf{Computational Communication}, I aim to uncover how speech reflects and shapes the ways people understand and relate to one another.
